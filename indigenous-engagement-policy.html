DRAFT — FOR BOARD APPROVAL

Indigenous Engagement & Knowledge Governance Policy

This draft is intended for Board review. It does not become policy until formally adopted by Turtle Island Land Trust.

1. Purpose

This policy is intended to guide how TILT enters, develops and communicates relationships with Indigenous Nations, communities and knowledge holders in connection with restoration, stewardship, research, education, landholding and governance.

The purpose is to support relationships that are early, Nation-specific, consent-based, respectful of Indigenous laws and protocols, and protective of knowledge, privacy and community authority.

2. Core principles

TILT will seek to work according to the following principles:

  • relationship before intervention;
  • Nation-specific engagement rather than a pan-Indigenous approach;
  • respect for Indigenous laws, governance and protocols;
  • free, prior and informed participation in decisions affecting a Nation's territory, knowledge or representation;
  • no assumption that participation equals endorsement;
  • knowledge governance, privacy and confidentiality by agreement;
  • meaningful opportunities for governance participation where a Nation wishes to participate;
  • reasonable support for participation so engagement does not create an avoidable financial barrier.

3. Early engagement

Where a potential project concerns an Indigenous Nation's traditional territory, knowledge, governance or participation, TILT should seek engagement before major restoration, acquisition, public-communication or governance decisions are treated as settled.

TILT should not present a completed plan and treat consultation as a request for endorsement.

4. Nation-specific protocols

TILT will not assume that one protocol, term, decision-making structure or knowledge framework applies across all Indigenous Nations. The appropriate relationship should be developed with the Nation involved.

Where the Nation identifies its own engagement, cultural, data or knowledge-governance requirements, those requirements should guide the project to the extent legally and operationally possible.

5. Consent and representation

TILT should not describe a Nation as a partner, supporter, co-governor or knowledge contributor without clear authorization. Attendance at a meeting, informal discussion or participation by an individual does not by itself authorize TILT to represent that the Nation has endorsed a project.

TILT does not determine who speaks for a Nation. Representation or nomination should be identified through a process supported by the Nation itself.

6. Knowledge governance

Indigenous knowledge, cultural information, sensitive locations, stories, community data and other information do not become TILT property simply because they are shared or inform a project.

Project agreements should establish, where relevant, who may collect, hold, access, interpret, reproduce, publish, archive or destroy information. Knowledge may guide project decisions without being disclosed publicly.

7. Ways of knowing

TILT's approach may be informed by Etuaptmumk (Two-Eyed Seeing), while recognizing that Etuaptmumk is Mi'kmaw and does not replace the distinct laws, knowledge systems or protocols of other Nations.

Western scientific methods and Indigenous knowledge should not be placed in a hierarchy in which one is required to validate the other. Each may contribute according to the permissions and context of the project.

8. Privacy and safety

TILT will not publish names, photographs, biographies, Nation affiliations, personal contact information or details of participation without consent, subject to legal disclosure requirements.

Where lawful and practical, TILT should use service addresses rather than residential addresses in public corporate records and minimize unnecessary republication of personal information.

9. Participation support

TILT intends, where resources permit, to cover reasonable travel, accommodation and necessary meeting costs associated with Indigenous participation in TILT governance or project work.

TILT will seek not to rely on unpaid Indigenous expertise. Compensation for project, cultural, advisory, professional or other eligible services may be provided where funding and applicable charity law permit and where those services are distinct from any uncompensated statutory director duties.

10. Governance participation

Where TILT has an active project within a Nation's traditional territory, the Trust's goal is to seek meaningful Board or project-governance participation connected to that Nation where the Nation wishes to participate.

The form of participation may differ by project and should be developed with the Nation rather than imposed by TILT.

11. Public communications

Public communications should distinguish between information TILT is authorized to share and information that remains confidential or restricted. TILT should not use Indigenous knowledge, cultural material, imagery or Nation identity as branding or evidence of legitimacy without permission.

12. Review and accountability

This policy should be reviewed after the first substantive Indigenous partnership and thereafter at intervals determined by the Board. The Board should invite participating Nations or Indigenous advisors to identify where the policy should change based on experience.

Status

Draft for Board approval.

This document is not yet an adopted TILT policy. Comments or proposed revisions can be considered before Board approval.
