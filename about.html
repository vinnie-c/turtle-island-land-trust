Turtle Island Land Trust

About Us

We restore degraded places, protect places of lasting value, and connect stewardship to research, education and public participation.

Our approach

Restore. Protect. Learn.

The Trust is built around long-term care for place. Restoration addresses damage, conservation protects what should endure, and learning makes the work useful beyond the boundaries of a single property.

01

Restore

Rehabilitate degraded, contaminated, vacant or abandoned places and recover ecological function, biodiversity and water quality where possible.

02

Protect

Acquire, conserve, maintain and steward lands, buildings and places where long-term protection creates a clear public benefit.

03

Learn

Use research, monitoring, field learning, workshops, publications and public programs to share what restoration and stewardship can teach us.

Our mission

Turtle Island Land Trust protects, restores and stewards natural and urban places for public benefit, while advancing environmental education, ecological restoration and biodiversity conservation.

Where we work

The Trust is Canadian in scope. Early opportunities are expected to focus on Ontario, Nova Scotia and British Columbia, with growth guided by ecological need, public benefit and organizational capacity.

What counts as a place

Our work is not limited to remote landscapes. A place may be a rural property, wetland, shoreline, urban lot, heritage site or building where restoration, conservation, education or charitable use can create lasting value.

Accountability

We are building project reporting around clear descriptions of the place, the problem being addressed, the action taken, the partners involved and the evidence available over time. Governance and financial transparency will grow alongside the Trust’s activities.

Governance

Stewardship starts with accountability.

Turtle Island Land Trust is a federal not-for-profit corporation governed under the Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act. The Board of Directors provides oversight of the Trust’s purposes, finances, stewardship responsibilities and long-term direction.

Indigenous governance

Governance rooted in place.

Where the Trust undertakes a restoration project within the traditional territory of an Indigenous Nation, our goal is to invite that Nation into the project’s governance from the outset and to seek an Indigenous director connected to that Nation, where the Nation wishes to participate.

Restoration is intended to support long-term Indigenous stewardship. Where appropriate, agreed with the Nation, and legally permissible, the Trust will work toward the transfer or return of restored land or stewardship interests once ecological objectives and transfer conditions are met.

01

Territorial representation

For each active project territory, the Trust seeks meaningful Board participation by an Indigenous person connected to the Nation whose traditional territory includes the project. Introductions and nominations from Nations and communities are welcome.

02

Privacy by consent

Names, photographs, biographies, Nation affiliations and personal contact information are not published on this website without explicit consent. Where legally available, a service address can be used for corporate filings instead of a residential address. Statutory public disclosure requirements still apply.

03

Participation support

The Trust intends to cover reasonable travel, accommodation and necessary meeting costs so participation does not create a financial barrier. We are also committed to not relying on unpaid Indigenous expertise.

04

Respect for expertise

Where funding and applicable charity law permit, compensation may be provided for eligible project, cultural, advisory, professional or other services that are separate from a director’s statutory Board duties.
