Projects & Education

Our Work

We organize the work by setting — rural or urban — and by what is happening there: restoration, conservation, land stewardship, building rehabilitation and public learning.

Project reporting

Every project should make the case for the work.

As TILT projects are formally established, their profiles will document the place, the need, the intervention and the evidence available over time.

01Place

Location, setting and why the site matters.

02Need

The ecological, heritage or public-benefit problem being addressed.

03Intervention

The restoration, conservation, rehabilitation or education action being taken.

04Evidence

Monitoring, outcomes, partners and long-term stewardship as information becomes available.

Project taxonomy

A simple way to read the portfolio.

Projects can carry more than one tag. The tags describe the kind of work; Rural and Urban describe the setting.

RestorationRepair degraded places and recover ecological function.
ConservationProtect places of lasting ecological or community value.
LandLand, habitat, shorelines, wetlands and watersheds.
BuildingsBuildings restored or adapted for charitable public benefit.

Rural Projects

Land, water and habitat.

Rural work can include degraded lands, habitats, wetlands, shorelines and watersheds where restoration or long-term protection can create lasting public benefit.

Work stream
RestorationLand

Ecological restoration

Habitat rehabilitation, biodiversity recovery, water and shoreline restoration, invasive-species management, native planting and monitoring.

Work stream
ConservationLand

Long-term conservation

Acquisition, protection and stewardship of lands and interests in land where ecological or public value should endure over time.

Urban Projects

Land and buildings in cities.

Urban projects can include vacant or underused land, habitat, heritage places, community spaces and buildings adapted for restoration, conservation, education or other charitable public uses.

Work stream
RestorationBuildings

Building rehabilitation

Buildings can be restored, conserved or adapted to support educational programming, charitable activities, community use and stewardship.

Work stream
RestorationConservationLand

Urban land restoration

Vacant or degraded urban land can be restored for biodiversity, ecological function, education and community connection.

Education & Learning

Learning is part of the work.

Restoration and conservation sites can support structured learning, research and public programs that make ecological knowledge useful beyond the project itself.

01 Land-based and ocean-based learning
02 Courses, workshops and public programs
03 Research, monitoring and publications
04 Talks, exhibitions and educational places
